Food, and the four buildings that make it

Soldiers should march on bread and bacon, not on raw wheat. That splits one good into a chain, and the chain needs four buildings the free KayKit Medieval Hexagon pack was never going to hand us whole. What this does to the barracks

Wheat stops being a war material and goes back to being a crop: it feeds the mill, and nothing else. Food is what a soldier costs, which means an army needs a working food chain behind it rather than one farm — and it gives the fishery a reason to exist on a coastal map where the wheat belt is somebody else’s.

Built, and down to three buildings. Flour and food are goods; the windmill grinds wheat, the bakery bakes flour and water into bread, the shipyard fishes a coastline for nothing at all, and the barracks trains on bread — the ledger above is live. Two ways to feed an army, priced against each other: the fishery is early and slow — one hut, one hand, nothing going in — and the bread chain is late and fast: a well, a farm, a mill and a bakery before its first loaf, then three times the rate. A hen yard was the third, turning wheat straight into food, and it was cut: it lost to the bakery on every number that mattered, and two food sources are a choice where three were a menu. The fishery also brought the game its first placement rule that is not about ore — it has to touch water, and it turns to face it.

The cards below are still the drawing board: the alternatives that did not win, and what each would have cost. Swapping one in is a line or two — the compositions here are the code that would ship. The bakery is the one I would revisit first, since it went in as the bake-house and the oven house is the better silhouette.

The four EXTRA models the fishery leans on — shipyard, docks, anchor, boat rack — are in the checkout under models/kaykit/extra/, which is why its hand-built column is down to a sign post. The rest of the pack is not: drop its Assets/gltf folder in beside them and say so, and the remaining hand-built parts can go the same way. Every card here is one line of a recipe list, so re-cutting one against a real model is quick.
